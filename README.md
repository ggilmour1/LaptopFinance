# Laptop Finance
## Aim

The aim of this application is to teach players about different ways of paying for big purchases.

## Objectives

Players will be given the scenario that their laptop computer has just died.  They will be given a number of options:

1. There will be 3 options for the new laptop, with differing costs.
2. How to finance it.
3. How smooth the journey will be.

Each scenario will be played out over 2 years (24 months), and there will be interactive decision points during the game. 

At the end of each game, the player will see what their selection cost them, both financially, and in terms of their credit rating.

## Game design

The game has been built mainly using Claude Sonnet 5 AI, This has been built using multiple itterations to enhance each stage, making the game play more refined. 

## Game play

### Laptop selection

Players can choose the model they want to buy from: 

1. Budget laptop, costing £800.00, or £45.00 per month. It is a basic machine, but good enough
2. A standard laptop, costing £1,200.00, or £60.00 per month.  It is a solid machine, ideal for work or school. 
3. A premium laptop, costing £2,000.00, or £85.00 per month. Top of the line and super fast. 

### Interest rates

The gamne offers three scenario's, reflecting different financial markets

1. Favourable: Friendly banks, good credit, low rates.  Loans 6.0%, credit cards 18.0%
2. Standard rates: Typical market rates.  Loans 9.0%, credit cards 24.0%
3. Tough rates: Challenging times.  Loans 14.0%, credit cards 29.0%

### Income stability

This offers 2 choices:

1. Steady income.  No risks, predictable income every month
2. Job loss shock. Risk of losing your job and having no income for a month or more. 

## Play mode

Decide to manually select from these choices, or to roll the dice and take a random choice. The game will play out over the 2 years based on which option you select. 

### Finance options

The options presented to the player will be:

1. Save for the laptop.  You wont have the laptop till you have saved enough.
2. Take out a loan.  The payments are fixed and predictable, so easier to budget. 
3. Use a credit card.  This is the most flexible, but also the most expensive option.  You can pay back a smaller amout each month, but that means you might pay a lot more for it in the end. 

### Changing payment amounts

YOu can select, at various points in the game, to pay more to your card.  Also, if you don't have enough money, you can choose to use a backup card, use an overdraft, or miss a payment.  Each of these have consequences too. 

## Ending

At the end of the game, you are shown how you did, based on your choice.  The other two choices are also displayed, showing what could have happened if you had selected them.  There is also and analysis and advice on the consequences of your choices.
