A Financial Intelligence Game

The Ledger

One laptop. Three ways to pay for it. Twenty-four months to find out what it really cost you.

The Scenario

You need a $1,200 laptop.

Yours just died. You need one for work or school. You have no savings toward it yet, and about $60/month of spare cash you could put toward this if you choose to save. Pick how you'll pay for it — then live out 24 months and see what each choice actually costs.

Save First

Set the $60/month aside in a savings account. Buy the laptop once you've saved enough. You wait — but you earn interest, and you owe no one.

Savings APY: 3.0%
Deposit: $60/mo
Laptop arrives: when funded

Personal Loan

Borrow $1,200 from the bank today, get the laptop immediately, repay in fixed monthly installments over 2 years.

APR: 9.0%
Term: 24 months
Laptop arrives: today

Credit Card

Charge $1,200 to your credit card, get the laptop immediately. Pay it down however fast (or slow) you like each month.

APR: 24.0%
Term: open-ended
Laptop arrives: today
